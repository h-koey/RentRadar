Part 5: Deployment, Cost, and Production Readiness

5.1 Deployment: Our group has selected PythonAnywhere to host Illini Rent Radar.

5.2 Infrastructure Requirements:

    - We would likely have to change the ollama model to a more lightweight model.
    It is important to acknowledge that the average user of this site might have an
    older laptop or phone. In this case, they would lack the RAM. As a result, we should
    aim for smaller model or utilize a self-hosted model. In the future, we can test out
    which models perform the best and compare their computational cost. The AI infrastructure
    should cater to the average student, thus, we'd likely sacrifice some degree of accuracy
    for speed.

    -The bulk of our app is individual apartment listings. We want to be very intentional
    with what we include with the goal of limiting storage. The main goal is to ensure the
    prediction feature and apartment analytics don't suffocate the project.

    - For this project, we are going to stick with sqlite3 because it is the free option.
    If Illini Rent Radar gains a significant user base, we would likely upgrade our hosting
    software subscription and incorporate Postgres. Sqlite3's limited writing and scalability
    would become a problem if we have over 10 thousand users.

5.4: Scalability:
    1. At 10,000 users, we would start to consider upgrading our deployment subscription. A userbase
    this large would start to expose the limitations of sqlite3. If we continue to see growth, we would
    look into different DBMSs like Postgres. With our current architecture, the prediction and analytic features
    would likely be the cause of bottlenecking. If ~10,000 users are making requests to the Ollama api, it'll
    result in high costs and incredibly poor performance.

    2. Hundreds of requests to the LLM at once has the potential to bottleneck our app with the current infrastructure.
    In addition, we need to be cautious of the pages where we display analytics. Having a live analytical look on most
    of our app's pages will quickly cause a bottleneck if there are thousands of concurrent users.

    4. To maintain application performance, we could develop a meaningful dashboard in our admin page.
    With this dashboard, we could monitor the amount of calls, monthly users, and trends in data. Furthermore,
    we can use django logging to catch errors early.

    5. Upgrading to a stronger DBMS (sqlite3 --> Postgres). In the future, our app can also utilize vertical or
    horizontal scaling to reduce costs. Horizontal scaling would probably be the most effective because we could easily
    adjust our architecture for spikes and drops. Due to the nature of our app, we will see spikes during "peak apartment search time."
    Having an adaptable architecture would be an effective way to cut cost.
